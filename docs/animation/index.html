Tonic and burst stimulation of a myelinated axon

Membrane voltage at every node of a 4 µm MRG axon model during spinal cord stimulation at 1.5× activation threshold, re-run from the EMBC 2025 study. Each biphasic pulse can start a spike near the electrode; watch where spikes begin, how they travel along the fibre, and which ones never arrive.

t = 0.0 ms
Fibre. A 100 mm, 4 µm axon built with the McIntyre–Richardson–Grill geometry: 243 nodes of Ranvier 412 µm apart, each internode made of MYSA, FLUT and six STIN myelin segments. The close-up shows the 13 mm around the electrode; node width and all vertical sizes are exaggerated for visibility.
Electrode. Two point-source contacts 0.5 mm above the fibre and 3 mm apart. During each phase one contact is cathodic and the other anodic. Bars at the nodes show the activating function: where the field pushes the membrane towards (up) or away from (down) firing.
Voltage. Node colour and glow follow the recorded membrane potential, from hyperpolarised through rest to an action potential. The map below plots the same voltage for all nodes over time; ticks mark spikes leaving the left and right ends of the fibre.

Data: re-simulation of the amplitude-sweep configuration (4 µm fibre, 0.2 ms tonic and FAST pulses, 1 ms burst pulses, active charge balance, 1.5× threshold) with PyFibers and NEURON 8.2.6. Thresholds, action-potential counts and last-spike times match the saved project outputs exactly. Paper: Atchuthan, Grill & Meijs, IEEE EMBC 2025. The model explores mechanisms; it does not predict clinical outcomes.
